What's the smallest neural network that still demonstrates something a linear
model can't do? About fifty lines of NumPy: a two-layer MLP with a single
hidden layer, trained with hand-written backprop on the classic *two moons*
dataset. No autograd, no framework — just matrix multiplies and the chain
rule.

## The model

The network is:

$$
\hat{y} = \sigma\!\left(\tanh(x W_1 + b_1)\, W_2 + b_2\right)
$$

with a hidden width of 16, optimized by gradient descent on binary
cross-entropy:

$$
\mathcal{L} = -\frac{1}{N}\sum_i y_i \log \hat{y}_i + (1 - y_i)\log(1 - \hat{y}_i)
$$

The full implementation lives in `src/lib/cli.py` and exposes flags for
sample count, noise, hidden width, epochs, learning rate, and seed.

## Does it work?

After 2000 epochs at learning rate 0.05 on 400 noisy points, final loss is
**0.255** and training accuracy is **88.2%** — about what you'd expect given
the noise floor of the dataset.

### Loss curve

The cross-entropy drops sharply in the first few hundred epochs and then
settles into a slow grind, which is the usual shape for a small MLP on a
small problem.

### Decision boundary, before and after

The interesting plot is the decision surface. At initialization the network
produces an arbitrary linear-ish split — it has learned nothing:

![Decision boundary at epoch 0](/boundary_before.png)

After training, the boundary curves between the two interleaved crescents.
This is the part a logistic regressor *cannot* do, which is why two-moons is
the standard sanity check for nonlinear classifiers:

![Decision boundary at epoch 2000](/boundary_after.png)

## Reproducing

```bash
python3 src/lib/cli.py --epochs 2000 --hidden 16 --lr 0.05
```

Plots are written to `src/docs/public/`, which is why they show up in this
post without any extra wiring.
